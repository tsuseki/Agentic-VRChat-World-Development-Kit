# Case study: a luxury rooftop residence, layout postmortem

For world-architect and layout-reviewer. A two-storey luxury residence hangout world, built once,
then remade at a smaller scale. Quotes are the world owner's recorded feedback. Anything deduced
rather than recorded is marked [INFERENCE]. Source: the maintainers' own project records.

## 1. The two "square box" episodes

There were **two** literal-square layouts, and the owner rejected the squareness both times.

**Episode A: original blockout.**
- The first house: ground floor a **40 × 40 m square plate (1600 m²)**, upper floor 40 × 30 m
  (1200 m²), 5 m clear storeys, on a 60 × 60 m site.
- 32 massing studies were produced and kept in an archive scene; one "Corner-L" variant was
  accepted: building in one corner, garden wrapping two sides, so the *site* reads as an L even
  though the building envelope stayed an extruded rectangle. [INFERENCE: the studies exist because
  the first single-box proposal didn't satisfy.]

**Episode B: the remake.**
- The remake's first shell was the old plan scaled ×0.625 into a **25 × 25 m square**.
- Owner verdict: **"boring square"** → make the envelope more dynamic (cut corners, extensions;
  not abstract art); "rooms stay mostly the same, only the general building shape changes"; the
  owner wanted **"cut corners combined with push & pull"** and **"F2 overhanging the jacuzzi
  and/or the bar"**.

## 3. Owner feedback (layout-relevant)

Original house:
- "Stair wall reads bad: the blank spandrel/side wall of the main stair. Rework it." Offender: a
  **continuous 24 m × 5 m blank plaster plane** behind the flight.
- "Shrink the wide opening (the big wall cut) so couches stop blocking it." A 4.00 m doorway was
  rebuilt as **2.40 × 3.00 m**, couch pulled 0.58 m clear of the jamb.
- "Potted plants must stand near a wall or near furniture, never alone mid-floor or mid-corridor."
  (70 of 75 interior plants failed.)
- "Inside is too cluttered; leave standing and walking space, not too much stuff."
- "Minimal floor level changes; flatten lounge pit/half-steps." The day-one sunken pit was removed;
  the design rules now say "No sunken pit or decorative half-steps."
- "Aquarium is in the way; only the flat intermediate stair landing was intended." (3.4 × 5.7 m →
  3.4 × 1.4 m.)
- Suites "must be unique, not mirrored copies": both rebuilt from single mirrored boxes into
  different plans.

Remake:
- Scale: "**Design reference person is 1.2 m tall, eye height ~1.1 m** (friend-group average)…
  **The world felt empty**: the shell is being rebuilt smaller while prop sizes stay unchanged."
- Plan v1: "**never present plans as ASCII; use rendered images**"; "Kitchen and dining stay
  separate rooms"; "do not over-rework F2".
- Plan v3: "**private rooms need more space to feel realistic**".
- Plan v4: "always present plans this way" (portrait, ground floor top, upper floor bottom); the
  owner asked whether 40 → 25 m is too much shrink.
- Plan v5: keep the "**wide hallway with couches in front of the private rooms**"; "the **book wall
  sits directly at the top of the stairs** as the threshold into the private floor".
- Plan v6: "doors drawn (**always draw doors**)"; the owner liked the current hallway composition,
  which was then **measured in the scene** piece by piece before redrawing.
- Plan v7: "**Direction reset: keep the CURRENT layouts and door arrangement, just smaller**"
  (scale 0.625 toward one corner; furniture stays real size).
- Plan v9: ground-floor doorways "a bit smaller"; every doorway got an explicit before → after
  width (lounge→entry 3.4 → 2.4 m, bar→big room 3.1 → 1.6 m, entry→big room 1.5 → 1.2 m…).
- Plan v10 lesson: "**draw circulation spaces as one outline and measure every feature wall from
  the scene before drawing**" (a drawing artefact was mistaken for a wall; "keep the fireside
  lounge wall" meant a freestanding fireplace wall the plan hadn't drawn).
- Plan v13: "the VR and server rooms are private, so they go upstairs; the gym is more public";
  gym entered "from the back" through a 1.2 m door from the wet bar (owner liked this).
- Massing: "**boring square**"; V6 picked "with changes: jacuzzi fully roofed… balcony less messy;
  remove the dent where the SW corner cut met the pavilion"; in V9 the pocket where the upper-floor
  cut met the bridge was "**an ugly hole**"; outdoor stairs **rejected** → "U-turn stairs inside
  the buildings".
- Gym sizing: "gym too big; **private residence, no public showers**"; then "no home office, one
  bathroom"; then "**some spa thingy, wellness stuff**" → wellness room.
- Go: "**ok build it**"; "**we're set on the design**".

Post-build, from owner screenshots:
- "**the chairs aren't really reachable**", "**looks like a hole**", "**it's floating stuff …
  rework this whole area**", "**make the whole hallway wider … the private rooms can be
  squished**" → corridor 2.2 → **3.1 m clear**, sealed bedrooms squashed to 4.75 m deep, floating
  ceiling raft replaced by wall-to-wall bulkheads.
- "**get rid of this wall between games and staircase**" → wall replaced by a plain metal handrail
  (games room open to the stair void).
- Bar stair: "**stairs and rail near the edge of the building**", "**a bit wider**" → 1.75 m wide,
  stringer against the glass.
- Guest suite: "**bathroom in a cage… make actual walls and remove the cage door**" → slatted pod
  → plaster walls to ceiling, cased doorway, no leaf; standing rule: no slatted "cage" pods or
  screens.

## 4. Before / after

**Before (accepted original, "Corner-L"):**
- Plate 60 × 60 m (3600 m²); ground floor 40 × 40 m (1600 m²); upper floor 40 × 30 m (1200 m²)
  plus a 40 × 10 m balcony; storeys **5 m clear**, 0.4 m slabs; stair void 8 × 7 m; garden L 2000 m².
- Ground floor: main lounge (+ under-stair aquarium), wet bar, dance/DJ, kitchen, dining, bathroom,
  chill (billiards), games (poker), entry/lift. Upper floor: mezzanine lounge, sky lounge,
  spa/sauna, VIP hallway (2 teleport doors + supporter board), gym, sealed shells, balcony. Suites
  = 2 remote teleport islands 243 m apart.
- Designed for a ~1.75 m person (1.6 m eye). **Failure: "The world felt empty"** at the group's
  actual 1.2 m avatar height.

**After (remake):**
- Plate 45 × 45 m (×0.75); building core 25 × 25 m (×0.625) **but no longer a square**: two 45°
  corner cuts, a two-storey **annex** beside the jacuzzi, an upper-floor **gym bridge** roofing the
  jacuzzi, a **bar wing** overhanging the outdoor bar with its last 6.25 m an open balcony on 2
  columns, a raised games roof with clerestory, main balcony on the ground-floor roof behind one
  clean 45° diagonal. Storeys **3.8 m clear**. Stairs: main straight stair inside the lounge,
  U-stair in the annex, open 1.75 m bar stair against the glass. Plan stored as room polygons in a
  data file read by the builder script.
- Ground floor: main lounge (main stair inside it), dance/DJ opening onto a covered porch, wet bar,
  fireside lounge (fireplace + piano), entry, bathroom, kitchen, dining, pool house. Upper floor:
  gym on the bridge (one machine row along the glass, visible from the garden), wellness room +
  bathroom + sauna/steam, library behind diagonal glass at the stair arrival, games room open to
  the stair void behind a handrail, hallway (teleport doors, supporter board), VR room, server
  room, two sealed bedroom fronts, sky lounge in the bar wing.
- Counters/built-ins scaled ~0.9; **loose furniture kept at real size**: the shell shrinks around
  the props.

## 5. Generalizable lessons for layout agents

Shape and massing
1. **A single extruded rectangle will be rejected.** Both the 40×40 and the 25×25 shells drew
   corrections; the accepted envelope needed cut corners + push/pull volumes + an overhang, but
   "not abstract art", and "rooms stay mostly the same": the envelope moves, the program doesn't.
2. **Generate massing variants side by side in one walls-only scene with the reference-person
   capsule** (variants at +60 m x-offsets, 1.2 m capsule, area labels). The owner picks a variant
   *with changes*; expect 3–5 refinement rounds on the pick (V6 → V7/V8 → V9 ×6 here).
3. **Junctions between volumes are where "ugly holes" and "dents" appear.** Two rejections were
   exactly that; resolved by running one clean 45° line corner to corner. Rule: every diagonal cut
   terminates on an existing corner; no leftover notches or pockets.
4. **Verticality beats footprint tricks:** the features that survived are sectional: bridge over
   water, wing on columns with a balcony end, raised roof with clerestory, double-height stair
   arrival. [INFERENCE]

Scale
5. **Anchor everything to a measured reference person.** The whole remake was triggered by a scale
   mismatch (1.75 m assumption vs 1.2 m real avatars). Non-uniform shrink: shell ×0.625, built-in
   millwork ×0.9, loose props ×1.0; storeys 5 m → 3.8 m. [INFERENCE: room volume scales with the
   avatar; props scale with their real-world identity.]
6. **Doorways:** after the shrink the owner still wanted them narrower; approved widths landed at
   **1.0–2.4 m** (private rooms 1.0–1.2 m, main portals 2.2–2.4 m) vs rejected 3.1–4.6 m openings.
   A 4 m opening read as a hole and collected furniture in its throat.
7. **Stairs oscillated 6.0 → 2.8 → 2.2 → 6.0 → 2.23 → 3.60 m** before settling (1.75 m for the
   secondary stair at 1.2 m scale). Both extremes failed: 6 m wasted floor and created a 24 × 5 m
   blank wall; 2.2 m stopped reading as the feature stair. [INFERENCE: feature stair ≈ 3× shoulder
   width of the reference person; secondary stairs ≈ 1.5×.]

Circulation and rooms
8. **Corridors are rooms.** The 2.2 m corridor failed ("make the whole hallway wider … the private
   rooms can be squished"); fixed at **3.1 m clear** plus a 4.6 m-deep junction hall with a sofa
   lounge. Sealed space was sacrificed first. Furnished hallway pockets (couches facing the doors
   they serve) were explicitly preserved through three plan versions.
9. **Dead-end views must terminate on something designed**: a corridor used to end on "a floating
   pier beside a dark hole"; fixed with a console + lamp + mirror face and a flat ceiling.
10. **Flat floors win.** The sunken lounge pit and half-steps were ordered flat; only wet-area
    podiums with smooth ramps survived.
11. **Reachability is a hard gate:** "the chairs aren't really reachable" is a blocker even when
    composition shots look good. The fix: a 0.84–0.85 m approach lane between angled chairs;
    teleport landings raycast-checked (floor hit, clear capsule, door 1.37 m behind).
12. **Public/private zoning drives placement:** ground floor = party, upper floor = lived-in home;
    "VR and server rooms are private, so they go upstairs; the gym is more public" (moved down,
    then onto the glass bridge *to be seen from the garden*). Program follows social exposure, not
    adjacency convenience.
13. **Sealed fake rooms are legitimate** (solid walls behind doors; suites as teleport islands
    ≥ 200 m out and ≥ 200 m apart), but their *fronts* must read as real rooms: "private rooms need
    more space to feel realistic" (door bays 6.5–8.25 m wide, ≥ 4.75 m apparent depth, real doors
    + lock panels).
14. **Preserve what the owner approved:** the v7 reset ("keep the CURRENT layouts and door
    arrangement, just smaller") shows owners anchor to layouts they already live in; wholesale
    re-planning was rejected twice.

Process (what the pipeline copies)
15. **Shotgun with cheap renders, one change-set per version.** 14 interior + 3 outside plan
    versions in one day, each a rendered PNG (portrait, ground floor above upper, doors drawn),
    each closing exactly the previous round's feedback. Never ASCII plans. Keep every version and
    encode the final one in the builder.
16. **Measure the live scene before redrawing it.** Two plan errors came from drawing from memory:
    an invented hallway wall and a missed freestanding fireplace wall.
17. **Make layout regenerable:** shell and site rebuilt by an editor builder script from room
    polygons in a data file; "change the layout in the builder, never by hand". Walls weld to one
    mesh per floor (bake seams); collision stays invisible boxes (215+ BoxColliders, no
    MeshColliders).
18. **Unsolicited layout reworks get reverted.** The "emptiness pass" that opened sealed shells
    into a library and karaoke room was rolled back the next day and became a standing prohibition
    ("do not turn an aesthetic note into an unsolicited room rebuild"). Layout changes happen only
    against explicit owner feedback; owner-reported failures override earlier passing checks.
19. **Pitfall symptoms to scan for** (all occurred): blank wall planes > ~15 m² facing a main route;
    furniture straddling doorways; props floating mid-circulation; mirrored-copy rooms; coplanar
    faces/z-fighting at wall junctions; invisible ceilings over voids; detached floating ceiling
    rafts; fixtures hanging inside full-height glass so cords read against the sky ("rods hanging
    from nothing"); slatted screens that read as cages.
