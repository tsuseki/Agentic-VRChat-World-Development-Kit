# UV2 quality

A lightmapped renderer needs a UV2 (Unity `mesh.uv2`, UV channel 1, named `Lightmap` in Blender
exports) that is valid, unfragmented and allocated by what players see. Bakery uses UV2 when present,
otherwise UV0. A repeating material UV is never a usable fallback.

## 1. Import settings

- **Generate Lightmap UVs on pack FBXs** that ship without UV2, before they go lightmap-static.
- **Margin method Manual, pack margin ~6** (`secondaryUVMarginMethod = Manual`,
  `secondaryUVPackMargin` 6: pixels for a mesh filling a 1024 map). Then set Scale In Lightmap per
  class.
  - The importer's Calculate margin at a low `secondaryUVMinLightmapResolution` (16) left UV2 fill at
    0.10–0.13; Manual 6 gave 0.39–0.46.
  - In a worse case Calculate collapsed the islands into a corner (texel extent 25 → 7,939).
- **Hard Angle and Area Error set the chart count.** The defaults (88°, 15 %) suit hard-surface
  models. For organic models raise Hard Angle toward 180° (every edge smooth) and Area Error
  moderately, to get fewer, larger charts. Then re-run the audit; if it still fails, author the UV2
  in Blender (§2).
- **Check that the unwrap actually ran on every mesh in the file.** The importer can silently leave
  one LOD0 without UV2 while LOD1/LOD2 get one.
- **Bakery's own UV processing:**
  - With Unwrap UVs on, Bakery reimports models during export. That can re-pad UV2 and can reload the
    domain mid-bake.
  - "Adjust UV padding" recalculates padding on auto-generated UVs and ignores the importer margin.
  - Decide one owner per asset: importer, Bakery or Blender. Use "Don't change" for assets with
    authored UV2.
- **ProBuilder:**
  - `Refresh()` builds no UV2; `EditorMeshUtility.Optimize(pb, true)` does.
  - Its pack margin is in UV space (20 = 0.02 UV). Write a widened margin back to
    `unwrapParameters.packMargin`, or the next regeneration undoes it.
  - Re-meshing destroys UV2: set `lightmapIndex = -1` until the next bake.
- **The Unity Quad has no UV2** (the Cube has): copy UV0 into UV2 on a saved mesh asset.

## 2. When to author UV2 in Blender instead

Author the lightmap UV in Blender (`blender-modeler`; trees: `lowpoly-tree-generation`) and import
with *Generate Lightmap UVs* off when any of these holds:
- the piece is hero furniture players sit or lie on, and the importer UV2 fails the audit;
- the mesh is organic or noisy (cushions, upholstery, sculptures, plush, bedding). An automatic unwrap
  cuts hundreds of tiny charts, and at ~56² texels most texels sit on chart edges;
- the mesh is multi-material and one material's UV2 share is far below its area share (§3);
- the mesh mixes cutout cards with solid parts (trees, plants): an area-weighted unwrap counts
  transparent card corners as surface;
- it is generated by a script whose UV0 is world-projected, tiling or spherical (not a valid layout).
  When a procedural generator's UV0 is already a bijective chart layout in local space, `uv2 = uv0`
  beats the unwrapper.

**Authoring rules:**
- **Few, large, flat charts.** Cut seams at hard edges and hidden boundaries; one ribbon per tube or
  limb chain, not one chart per face. Don't join charts across a corner only to save seams: a coarse
  chart spanning a corner bleeds light across it.
- **Allocate by visibility, not surface area.** Weight seen faces up and hidden ones (undersides,
  backs against walls, card corners) down.
- **Uniform density** within a weighted class.
- **Margins** sized for the texel sizes the world bakes at: 0.010 UV worked where 0.002 bled
  neighbours into every rim.
- **Shelf-pack** into 0–1 with one uniform scale, and lay charts out from the texture UV so a re-run
  gives the same layout.
- **Two-sided shells** pack front and back in separate regions, never mirrored onto each other.
- **Smooth before unwrapping.** A clean surface unwraps into few charts; a noisy scan or sculpt
  fragments.
- Re-read the exported FBX: channel names and order, UV2 inside 0–1, zero overlap (rasterise at
  2048²), and nothing changed except UV1.

## 3. Per-material area-share parity

For each submesh (material):

```
areaShare = worldArea(submesh) / worldArea(mesh)
uv2Share  = uv2Area(submesh)   / uv2Area(mesh)
```

- **Flag** a submesh with `areaShare ≥ 0.10` and `uv2Share < 0.05`. It sits in a few texels and bakes
  one flat colour unlike the parts beside it: trunk vs branches, legs vs top, frame vs cushion, pot vs
  plant.
- **Healthy** is `uv2Share / areaShare` between ~0.5 and ~2. Shares well above that are fine only
  where the extra goes to surfaces players look at closely.
- **Fix:** re-pack with weights (Blender), or split the starved part into its own renderer with its
  own Scale In Lightmap.

## 4. Validity and overlap

- **UV2 inside 0–1, finite, no NaN.** "Has UV2" is not the test (`uv2.Length == vertexCount` passed on
  every renderer in a broken group); the value range is.
  - One mesh with garbage UV2 made Bakery's packer fail for its whole group: about 900 renderers got
    infinite scale-offsets.
  - On a reused mesh asset always write the channel (`uv2 = ok ? w2 : null`). `Mesh.Clear()` keeps a
    stale UV2 channel, and `GenerateSecondaryUVSet` adds vertices at seams.
- **No overlapping charts.** Overlap means two surfaces share texels: one of them gets the other's
  light.
  - "Overlapping UVs" warnings on meshes ≥ ~1.3 m are usually gutters under padding + 1 texels. Widen
    the margin, then trim the scale back.
  - On small pieces the warning means the piece should not be lightmapped.
  - A draft-resolution warning is unproven until the final-resolution bake shows it in a crop.
- **Charts ÷ triangles ≈ 1.0 means duplicate coincident triangles** (≈ 0.5 is normal on boxy meshes).
  Dedupe, weld by position + normal + UV0, re-unwrap, rebake. Welding by position alone kills hard
  corners.
- **Merged meshes** need either per-source UV2 transformed by each source's scale-offset (post-bake
  merge) or a fresh unwrap (pre-bake merge).

## 5. Organic and soft meshes

Choose one, per piece:
1. **Don't lightmap it** (default for small soft decor): P mode with its shadow kept in the bake
   (SKILL.md §1).
2. **Flat-chart unwrap** in Blender for large soft furniture (sofa bodies, mattresses, big cushions
   fused to a frame):
   - a handful of charts following the panels of the upholstery;
   - smoothed first;
   - then Scale In Lightmap until the audit passes (≤ 30 % triangles under 0.01 texel², ≤ 50 % of charts
     under 4 texels).

Never answer a fragmented UV2 with a higher Scale In Lightmap: the chart count stays, and the atlas
cost grows with the square of the scale.
