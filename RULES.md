# Hard rules — <workspace>

These stay in force for the whole session. Background and rationale live in `.omp/AGENTS.md`.

1. **Read the project's own `AGENTS.md` before editing that project.** Per-project files
   (`<project>/AGENTS.md` in each world project) do not auto-load from the workspace root.
   Read `DESIGN.md` and `TASKS.md` too when they exist.

2. **No colliders on small props; big furniture is solid.** Small furniture, decoration and clutter
   are walked through: chairs, stools, couches, armchairs, coffee/side/bedside tables, decor. Add a
   collider only where the player must not pass, prefer one `BoxCollider`, never a `MeshCollider` on
   anything walked on. Stairs get one smooth rotated ramp box, never per-tread collision. Anything
   created from a Unity primitive: destroy its auto-added collider.
   **Big furniture gets a solid collider:** dining and kitchen tables and other big tables, kitchen
   counters and islands, bar counters, cabinets, sideboards, dressers, wardrobes, bookcases and
   appliance-sized pieces get one always-on child `SolidCollider` (one `BoxCollider`) over their
   footprint, floor to top surface, so players can't walk through and pickups rest on them.
   **Lie-down colliders:** beds, couches and lie-on furniture get a top-surface child
   `LieDownCollider` (one `BoxCollider`), disabled by default and switched on only by the world's
   single lie-down colliders toggle (Udon). Requested pickups keep their own tight collider.
   **No barrier colliders; trunks always:** no invisible walls, world-edge, shore or coast
   boundaries, sea gates or wading walls, and no colliders on fences, railings or low walls.
   Players may walk into water, step off edges and jump over fences; the respawn height catches
   falls. Walk surfaces, building walls, rocks, big furniture and **every tree trunk** (one
   capsule fitted to the visible trunk; prop/pillar roots count) keep colliders. Every building
   players can see is enterable from the path that leads to it.
   **Jumping is on in every world:** one local UdonSharp movement-settings script per world sets
   `SetJumpImpulse(3)` (VRChat's default is 0) with the default walk/run/strafe/gravity.

3. **Never add a seat station unless the user explicitly asks.** No `VRC_Station`/`VRCStation`
   on any chair, sofa, bench, stool, bed, jacuzzi or prop, in any world. Strip stations off
   imported prefabs. Never re-add one that was removed. See `.omp/AGENTS.md`.

4. **The stage reviewers are the gate, not a courtesy.** Nothing built, generated, placed, textured
   or lit is "done" until the matching reviewer (`layout-` / `dressing-` / `final-reviewer`)
   returns SHIP IT (no blocker or should-fix finding, score ≥ 8). Your own screenshots and numbers
   do not count. Brief it with the stage, art direction, exact target and concerns. NEEDS WORK /
   MINOR FIXES is your next task list, not a report to relay. Whole worlds follow the
   `world-pipeline` skill. Exception: A.3 shell layouts, A.3 style-track images and style rounds are accepted by the
   **user's pick**; layout-reviewer only ranks them, and there is no SHIP IT loop before A.4.

5. **Drive the open Unity Editor through the Unity MCP, never the files.** Use the Unity MCP
   `execute_code` tool (and friends). Never hand-edit an open scene's YAML — the
   editor's in-memory state clobbers it. Scripted hierarchy edits need `MarkSceneDirty` before
   saving. After any script change, read the console and check for `error CS` before proceeding.

6. **Bake reflection probes immediately after every lighting bake.** A lightmap bake invalidates
   them and no luminance metric catches it. Probes only capture renderers flagged
   `ReflectionProbeStatic` — a different flag from `ContributeGI`.

7. **These git repos are local-only backups. Never push.** Commit a checkpoint after a
   significant chunk of work; `git add` can take minutes, so run it backgrounded.

8. **People are 1.4 m tall (eye 1.3 m) in all worlds; everything new is ×0.8 of real-world size.** Use a
   1.4 m person for every scale figure, camera and brief. Only collision minimums (2.3 m clear height,
   2.1 m doors, 2.0 m paths, 0.91 m stairs) never shrink. Details: spatial-design §5.9.

9. **Once the user confirms a 3D blockout, never go back to 2D plan rounds.** Changes to a
   confirmed shell or greybox (and to anything built after it) are made in 3D on the scene, shown
   from eye height and reviewed there; plan data is updated from the scene. Furniture is never
   planned in 2D or as greybox placeholders: it is placed in 3D in C.1 and recorded afterwards.

10. **Never model complex objects in Unity from primitives or generated meshes.** Unity-side
    geometry (primitives, or meshes generated by scripts and data generators) is for **boxes
    only**: slabs, decks, walls, plinths, quays, beams, square legs and towers, stair treads and
    landings, greybox massing. Every other shape comes in as an imported prefab, made to match the
    picked concept art, in this order:
    1. **`asset-finder` first**: the local library, owned packs and free sources (tanks, pipes,
       trusses, rails, ladders, machinery, statues, plants …);
    2. **`blender-modeler`** for simple geometric non-box pieces nothing fits (cylinders, tanks,
       domes, pipes, flanges, valve wheels, trusses and lattice frames, rails, caged ladders, drums,
       gates, cranes, cabins, waterfall ribbons), as a low-poly reusable kit;
    3. **Tripo image-to-3D** (`image-to-3d` tool) for organic or irregular shapes (crystals,
       complex machines, hulls, statues, rubble) from the picked concept sheets, cleaned in Blender
       (`blender-optimizer` / `blender-modeler`); trees by the tree skill.
    A faceted lathe, tube or lattice built in Unity is a blocker in review (user, 2026-11-03:
    "never model complex objects in unity using primitives, NEVER", "don't we have asset finder and
    blender modeler and tripo???").

11. **Test bakes are QUICK and never longer than 10 minutes.** Every test, iteration, fix-loop or
    review-round bake uses the QUICK preset (lowest samples, half texel density, bounces equal to
    final) and is cancelled if it runs past 10 minutes of wall time; shrink the scope and relaunch.
    Re-baking is fine. Only the final bake the user asks for may be slow. Details and the cancel
    procedure: `bakery-light-baking` "Baking rules" (user, 2026-11-05: "don't bake more than 10
    min", "you can do rebakes I meant just do low samples so a quick test doesn't take 1h").

12. **The user's decisions and picked art are binding: read them, record them, never override them.**
    Each project keeps a decision database, `<project>/STEER.jsonl`, and its generated digest
    `<project>/DECISIONS.md` (what holds now: picked art with absolute paths, rejected art, the
    design lock, every decision labelled by world part, aspect and target). Before working on a
    project, read `DECISIONS.md` for the parts you touch and open the picked images for them. A
    recorded decision beats workspace defaults, skill defaults (a skill's "pickups get gravity"
    loses to the project's "fumos have no gravity") and briefs; if a task contradicts one, stop and
    ask. The moment the user decides, picks or rejects something, record it in their exact words
    before acting: `python .omp/scripts/steer.py --project <project> add|pick ...` (`pick` copies
    liked art to `Design/picks/`, rejected art to `Design/picks/rejected/`), with `--aspect`
    (size, layout, design, style, …) and `--target` (objects, scripts, scene paths). Briefs to
    subagents quote the decisions and image paths they depend on. Details: `.omp/AGENTS.md`
    "User decisions and picked art" (user, 2026-11-05: "persistent important goals and decisions
    like the concept art and all the kind of user made decisions").
