---
title: Exploration landmarks pay off at close range — review them from the arrival viewpoint
topic: placement-layout-design
tags: [landmark, exploration, composition, review, viewpoint]
summary: If a landmark is a reward for walking up to it, judge it at 2–15 m and from where the player arrives; distance silhouette is optional.
---
**When:** hidden or optional landmarks in an open map (shrines, wrecks, monoliths) meant to be discovered.

**Rule:**
- The close-range payoff carries the whole landmark, so a weak read **at the reference eye height in WORLD.md** is a blocker. Example: a stone circle read as a ring only from ~3 m elevation and collapsed into scatter at 1.6 m eye height (that world's full-size reference). Fix it by tightening the circle, varying stone heights so the far arc closes, and giving the interior a focus.
- Being visible from the main route is fine (it's the invitation to go there); being right beside it is not. ~60 m+ from the road edge was plenty. Don't push landmarks far out just for distance.
- Don't "fix" a small far silhouette with beacons or taller masses unless distance readability is actually a goal. Tell reviewers which it is.
- Avoid placing landmarks on round coordinates if the terrain is tiled on that pitch — they end up on tile seams (see [tiled-terrain-low-texel-density-kills-contact-shadows](tiled-terrain-low-texel-density-kills-contact-shadows.md)).
